Cleaning tips

Simple ways to get your kitchen and bathroom sparkling with things you probably already have in the cupboard.

Kitchen tips Limescale tips

Around the kitchen

Steam the microwave clean

Heat a microwave-safe bowl of water with a sliced lemon for three to four minutes. Leave the door shut for five more, then wipe. The steam loosens the splatters so they come off easily.

You'll need: a bowl, water, a lemon

Shiny taps and sink

Hull's water is hard, so taps and sinks dull quickly. Wipe them dry after use, and once a week polish with a little white vinegar on a cloth, then rinse.

You'll need: white vinegar, microfibre cloths

Freshen the fridge

Wipe the shelves with warm water and a spoonful of bicarbonate of soda rather than strong cleaners, then leave an open pot of bicarb at the back to soak up smells.

You'll need: bicarbonate of soda, warm water, a cloth

Degrease the extractor filter

Metal extractor filters can go in the dishwasher, or soak in very hot water with washing-up liquid and a spoon of bicarb. Check yours is a metal one first.

You'll need: hot water, washing-up liquid, bicarbonate of soda

Bathroom & limescale

Wrap the taps

For crusty limescale round tap bases, soak kitchen roll in white vinegar, wrap it round the area and leave it for an hour. Scrub gently with an old toothbrush and rinse well. Skip this on natural stone like marble, which acid can damage.

You'll need: white vinegar, kitchen roll, an old toothbrush

Bag the shower head

Fill a sandwich bag with white vinegar, tie it round the shower head with an elastic band so the head sits in the vinegar, and leave it a couple of hours. Run the water for a minute before using it.

You'll need: white vinegar, a sandwich bag, an elastic band

Brighten the grout

Make a paste of bicarbonate of soda and water, work it into grout lines with an old toothbrush, leave ten minutes and rinse.

You'll need: bicarbonate of soda, an old toothbrush

Stop mould coming back

Open a window or run the fan during and after showers, squeegee the screen and tiles when you get out, and hang towels to dry outside the bathroom if you can.

You'll need: a shower squeegee

Around the house

Work from the top down

Dust high shelves, lights and skirting before you hoover, so anything that falls gets picked up last. Do the floors at the end, starting from the far side of the room and working toward the door.

You'll need: a duster or microfibre cloth, a hoover

Streak-free windows

Clean windows on a dry, cloudy day. Sun dries the water too fast and leaves streaks. Use warm water with a splash of washing-up liquid, a squeegee, and dry the edges with a microfibre cloth.

You'll need: a squeegee, warm water, washing-up liquid, microfibre cloths

Freshen a mattress

Sprinkle bicarbonate of soda over the mattress, leave it for an hour or so, then hoover it up with the upholstery tool.

You'll need: bicarbonate of soda, a hoover with an upholstery tool

Wash your cloths

Wash microfibre cloths at 60°C without fabric conditioner, which clogs the fibres and stops them picking up dirt.

You'll need: a washing machine

Stay safe

  • Never mix bleach with vinegar, limescale remover, toilet cleaner or anything containing ammonia. Together they give off toxic fumes.
  • Open a window when you clean, especially in small bathrooms.
  • Wear rubber gloves with strong cleaners and bleach, and keep all products away from children and pets.
  • Try any new method on a small hidden patch first.
